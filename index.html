Common questions

Why is VAT set on each line rather than on the invoice?

Because one job is regularly more than one rate. Fitting insulation is zero rated while the scaffolding to reach it might not be; a renovation of a dwelling empty for two years is reduced rated while a repair next door is standard rated. In the United States it is even starker: in many states the materials are taxable and the labour is not, on the same invoice. An app with a single rate box gets those jobs wrong, so Daywork puts the rate on the line.

What is the reverse charge, and when does Daywork use it?

Since 1 March 2021, when a VAT-registered subcontractor bills a VAT-registered contractor for construction services within CIS, the subcontractor charges no VAT and the customer accounts for it instead (VAT Act 1994 s55A). Daywork applies it when your customer has a VAT number, is registered for CIS as a contractor, has not told you they are an end user, and the job is construction operations — and prints the wording HMRC requires. If any one of those is not true, it charges VAT normally and tells you which one it was.

My customer says they are an “end user”. What do I do?

An end user is the person the work is ultimately for — they are not selling it on. Their confirmation switches the reverse charge off, and you go back to charging VAT as normal. HMRC expects that confirmation in writing, so tick Has confirmed they are an end user on the customer, record the date, and keep their email.

Why does the CIS deduction only come off part of my invoice?

Because that is the rule, and it is the single most valuable thing in the app. A contractor deducts 20% (or 30% if you are not registered) from the labour element only. Materials, consumable stores, plant hire and the fuel for it are taken off first, and VAT is never in the base at all. If you bill a job as one lump sum with no materials shown, HMRC's default is to treat the whole payment as labour — which means paying 20% on your parts as well as your time. Break the materials out.

Is plant hire materials or labour?

Plant hire on its own is treated with materials and stays out of the deduction. Plant hire with an operator is labour and goes into it. Daywork defaults an expense line to materials and lets you change it per line, because only you know which one it was.

Why do my invoice lines add up differently from another app?

Daywork rounds every line to the penny on its own and makes the total the sum of those rounded lines. Some software adds the unrounded lines and rounds once at the end, which can leave the printed lines a penny or two away from the printed total — and a customer with a calculator always finds it. HMRC's VAT guide permits rounding each line-level calculation to the nearest penny, so this approach is both correct and the one that adds up on the page.

Markup or margin?

They are different numbers and mixing them up is expensive. A £100 part sold at 50% markup goes out at £150 and earns you a 33.3% margin, not 50%. Daywork prices from markup, because that is how merchants quote, and shows you the margin next to it.

Can I take a deposit and bill in stages?

Yes, with Pro. A deposit reduces the final balance once you have actually invoiced it. Progress claims are cumulative — claim 3 is “the contract is 60% complete” less everything certified before — so the last claim always closes the job out exactly. Retention is held back as a percentage with a release date, and the VAT on retained money is not due until the retention is actually paid.

Why does ageing start from the due date?

Because ageing from the invoice date makes every invoice on 30-day terms look a month late the day you send it, which trains you to ignore the report. Daywork buckets from when each invoice actually fell due: current, 1–30, 31–60, 61–90 and 90+.

Can it chase people for me?

It drafts the message and you send it. The wording escalates on a ladder — a friendly note on the day, a chase at a week, a firmer reminder at a fortnight, a final notice at a month — and for a commercial debt it works out the statutory interest and fixed compensation you are entitled to under the Late Payment of Commercial Debts (Interest) Act 1998. Daywork never sends anything by itself and has no email account of its own.

Why does it ask me for the Bank of England base rate?

Because statutory interest is base rate plus 8%, and Daywork makes no network requests at all — so it cannot look the rate up. Set it once in Settings and the estimate will be right until the rate moves.

Will my invoice numbers have gaps in them?

No. A VAT invoice must carry a sequential number that uniquely identifies it, and a run with holes in it is the first thing an inspection asks about. Daywork issues numbers gap-free, never reuses one even after you delete an invoice, and has a gap check in Settings › Invoice numbering so you can see the run.

How do I cancel?

Subscriptions are managed by Apple, not by this app: open the Settings app, tap your name, then Subscriptions. Cancelling stops the next renewal and you keep Pro until the period you have paid for ends.

The widget is not updating.

Remove it and add it again. If it still shows nothing, open the app once — the widget reads a small file the app writes, and there is nothing to read until the app has run after installing.

Can I move my data to another app?

Yes. Pro exports invoices, invoice lines, payments, the tax summary and the aged report as CSV from Money › Export. Do it before you delete the app; there is no server-side copy.
