The short version

Daywork collects no personal data. There is no account, no sign-up and no analytics. The app makes no network requests of any kind. Your customers, your prices, your margins and your takings stay in Daywork's own storage on your iPhone, and nobody — including the developer — can see them.

What Daywork collects

Nothing. In App Store Connect terms, the app's data collection is declared as Data Not Collected, and that declaration is accurate rather than aspirational: the app contains no analytics framework, no advertising identifier, no crash reporter and no third-party SDK of any kind. It does not read your contacts, your photos, your location or your calendar, and it does not ask for permission to.

The only permission Daywork ever requests is notifications, and only if you leave chase reminders on. Declining it leaves the rest of the app working normally.

What stays on your device

Everything you enter: your business details, bank details, VAT and UTR numbers, your rates and price list, your customers and their contact details, every quote and invoice, what each job cost you, and what you have been paid. It is written to a single file inside the app's container, which iOS keeps private to Daywork, plus a shared App Group container holding a short summary so the widgets can display it.

This data is included in an encrypted iPhone or iCloud backup if you make one. That backup is between you and Apple; the developer has no access to it.

Your customers' data

Daywork holds names, addresses, phone numbers, email addresses and VAT numbers for people you work for. Under the UK and EU GDPR you are the controller of that information, not the developer — it is your business record, and none of it is ever transmitted anywhere.

Because the app has no network capability at all, the developer is not a processor of your customers' data either: nothing is received, stored or processed on your behalf. If a customer asks you to delete their details, you can do it inside the app, and the deletion is final. There is no copy anywhere else to chase.

A PDF you generate is created on your device. What happens to it once you share it — by email, message, or anything else — is between you, your customer and whichever app you used to send it.

What Apple sees

Purchases. If you subscribe to the Full Book, Apple processes the payment. The app receives only a signed receipt saying whether a subscription is active — never your name, your email or your payment details. Apple's handling of that transaction is covered by Apple's privacy policy.

App Store analytics. Apple reports aggregate, anonymised figures to developers, such as how many devices installed the app in a country. These cannot identify you and the app plays no part in producing them.

Your rights

Data protection law — including the UK and EU GDPR, and comparable laws elsewhere — gives you rights of access, correction, erasure, portability and objection over personal data held about you.

These rights are exercised against whoever holds the data. Because Daywork holds none, there is nothing for the developer to disclose, correct or delete. You already have complete and direct control: view and edit any record inside the app, export everything as CSV from Takings › Export, and erase the lot by deleting the app.

Keeping and deleting data

Your book is kept for as long as you keep the app installed, and no longer. Deleting Daywork removes its container and everything in it. There is no server-side copy to request the deletion of, and no way to recover a deleted file.

That matters more here than in most apps: in the UK you are generally required to keep business and VAT records for six years, and CIS records for at least three. Export your data before you delete the app, and keep the export somewhere you back up.

Security

Your data is protected by iOS's own file protection, which encrypts app data at rest while the device is locked. Setting a passcode, and turning on Face ID or Touch ID, is what makes that protection meaningful; an unlocked phone with no passcode protects nothing. Given that this app holds your bank details and your customers' contact details, that is worth doing.

Because no data is transmitted, there is no network channel to intercept and no server to breach.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it, and the previous wording stays in the public history of the repository that hosts this page. A change that materially affected how data is handled would be announced in the app before it took effect.
